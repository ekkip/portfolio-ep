Background
In spring 2023, I joined Till Nagel et al.'s project at HDILab, HS Mannheim, the aim of which was to find novel approaches to visualise smart city data in a geospatial AR context.
Details

We explored how temporal data can be embedded on flat surfaces in the city so that a visualisation stays tied to what people see around them. The aim was casual engagement with urban data on location—through a handheld AR view at a data experience point rather than a remote dashboard.

Our design concept describes four characteristics for these situated views: temporal directionality (how the flow of time reads from different standpoints), temporal situatedness (live, historic, or predictive data aligned with the moment of looking), spatial proportions (relating chart scale to real-world dimensions), and visualization idioms appropriate for street-level embedding.

We implemented a location-based mobile demonstrator at a central junction in Mannheim. Street-level traffic—cars, motorcycles, and trucks—was chosen for its visibility to passers-by; counts and categories came from computer-vision tracking installed on site as part of the local Smart City programme.

The demonstrator compares several embedded views of the same lane data—for example a grouped area chart, a ridge plot across two temporal granularities, and a stacked area chart—while labels stay readable toward the viewer as temporal direction shifts with perspective.
